What "Future of Work" means

Work isn't disappearing — it's being redefined.

AI, automation and constant change are reshaping what people do, how they do it, and the capabilities organizations need. Four shifts define the decade ahead.

Tasks are being automated

AI absorbs routine and repetitive work, so human value shifts toward judgment, creativity and relationships.

Doing tasks→Directing outcomes

Skills outrank titles

Careers are built on capabilities that transfer across roles, not fixed job descriptions that quickly age.

Job titles→Portable skills

Roles blend and evolve

New hybrid roles appear at the edges of old ones — combining human strengths with AI-assisted work.

Fixed roles→Hybrid roles

Reinvention is continuous

Learning no longer stops at graduation — people reskill and adapt across their whole working life.

Learn once→Reskill for life
The challenge

What organizations need from their people is changing faster than ever.

Skills for the future of work

The skills shaping the future of work.

Based on the World Economic Forum's Future of Jobs 2025 framework, PathKEY maps natural strengths against the skills organizations will increasingly need.

The goal isn't to be equally strong in every skill. It's to understand where potential already exists, and where development can have the greatest impact.

Analytical Thinking

Breaking complex ideas down to solve problems and make better decisions.

Resilience, Flexibility & Agility

Adapting, recovering and staying effective through pressure and change.

Leadership & Social Influence

Guiding and inspiring others, with or without formal authority.

Creative Thinking

Imagining possibilities and bringing new ideas into existence.

AI & Big Data

Applying AI and data to generate insight and inform decisions.

Empathy & Active Listening

Understanding others' perspectives and listening in ways that build trust.

Plus Systems Thinking, Curiosity & Lifelong Learning, and Talent Management — all mapped within the PathKEY Future of Work profile.
